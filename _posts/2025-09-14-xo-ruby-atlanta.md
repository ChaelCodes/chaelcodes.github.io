XO Ruby Atlanta was an intimate Ruby conference that took place at the Limelight Theater in Atlanta, Georgia on September 14, 2025. The single-day event brought together Ruby developers for a collection of inspiring talks, great networking, and the kind of warm community atmosphere that makes Ruby conferences special. The event was organized by @jremsikjr and the team at @flagrant.

<!--more-->

## The Conference Experience

The conference had a wonderful, intimate feel that made it easy to connect with fellow Ruby developers. The venue created a comfortable environment for both learning and networking, and the organizers did a fantastic job creating an inclusive and welcoming atmosphere.

## The Talks

The lineup of talks at XO Ruby Atlanta was fantastic, covering a range of topics that showcased both the technical depth and creative possibilities within the Ruby community.

### Scaling Postgres Beyond Query Optimization
**Speaker:** Alex Yarotsky

Alex talked about debugging the "curse of August" which plagued database performance at HubStaff. He covered postgres configs that have inefficient defaults, how autovacuum works, and more! His presentation was really engaging - he had some genuinely alarmed expressions when talking about database performance issues that hit close to home.

My thoughts: This was really good! The technical depth was perfect and Alex's delivery made complex database concepts accessible and entertaining.

*📺 Recording will be available at [RubyEvents](https://www.rubyevents.org/talks/scaling-postgresql-beyond-query-optimization)*

### Play with Your Code
**Speaker:** Rachael Wright-Munn (ChaelCodes)

I had the opportunity to present my "Play with Your Code" talk at XO Ruby Atlanta! This talk explores why programming games are more engaging than our day jobs and what lessons we can learn from them to improve our developer experience. We also dive into some fantastic programming games that every developer should try.

My thoughts: It was wonderful to share this talk with the XO Ruby Atlanta audience. The intimate setting made for great interaction and I loved seeing people get excited about discovering new programming games to play with. I really appreciate everyone who came up afterwards to tell me they liked it, and I'm looking forward to bringing a remixed version to SF Ruby!

*📺 Recording will be available at [RubyEvents](https://www.rubyevents.org/talks/play-with-your-code)*

![ChaelCodes presenting at XO Ruby Atlanta]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/chaelcodes-talk.jpg)
*Big shot of the theater and Chael standing at the podium next to a slide: Why are Programming Games fun? - Immediate Feedback, Color, Documentation, Purpose, Autonomy*

### The Disability Dilemma
**Speaker:** Kat

Kat talked about building empathy for hidden disabilities. Her presentation covered different conditions and how to respond with empathy rather than dismissiveness.

My thoughts: I felt a little called out by "Anxiety: I have to do something, right now" so I knew she was hitting something real. ❤️ This was an important and well-delivered talk about creating inclusive environments.

*📺 Recording will be available at [RubyEvents](https://www.rubyevents.org/talks/the-disability-dilemma)*

![Kat presenting about hidden disabilities]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/disability-talk.jpg)

### Empty Pipeline, Empty Future
**Speaker:** Thomas

This talk was about Thomas's experiences mentoring junior developers and it started so much conversation and dialog, because we all know we need junior developers to keep our community alive, but no one wants to pay for them.

My thoughts: The quotes "People need food and housing to learn" and "Juniors are an asset, not a liability" really resonated. This was a heartfelt and important discussion about the sustainability of our Ruby community.

*📺 Recording will be available at [RubyEvents](https://www.rubyevents.org/talks/empty-pipeline-empty-future)*

![Thomas presenting about mentoring junior developers]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/junior-dev-talk.jpg)
*"People need food and housing to learn" Thomas stands next to this absolute banger of a quote.*

### Overreacting: From React to Hotwire
**Speaker:** Igor Aleksandrov

Igor talked through why they moved from React to Hotwire and Stimulus and how it made it easier to ship code faster! He covered the bottlenecks React was creating for their team and how Hotwire simplified their development process.

My thoughts: Great practical insights about team dynamics and technology choices. The discussion about AI changing development expectations was particularly timely.

*📺 Recording will be available at [RubyEvents](https://www.rubyevents.org/talks/overreacting-from-react-to-hotwire)*

![Igor presenting about React to Hotwire migration]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/hotwire-talk.jpg)
*Igor speaks to the audience beside the slide about when AI changed the development game and new requirements for higher efficiency*

### Refactoring Volatile Views into Cohesive Components
**Speaker:** Jeremy Smith

I loved this talk by Jeremy about ViewComponents. It has valuable insights, even if you're not using it. His insights about view complexity, volatility, and churn are spot-on!

My thoughts: Even if you're not using ViewComponents, the framework for thinking about view complexity and when to extract components was incredibly useful.

*📺 Recording will be available at [RubyEvents](https://www.rubyevents.org/talks/refactoring-volatile-views-into-cohesive-components-xo-ruby-atlanta-2025)*

![Jeremy Smith presenting about ViewComponents]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/viewcomponents-talk.jpg)
*Jeremy describes how views become complex and volatile, covering Churn, Accumulation, Variation, and Proliferation*

### From Expensive Queries to Smart Caching: A DBA's Guide for Rails
**Speaker:** Javier Zon

Javier talked about issues he sees as a DBA and how to resolve them on the application side. His presentation included real-world examples and practical solutions.

My thoughts: Getting the DBA perspective on Rails applications was incredibly valuable. The real-world examples really drove home the impact of seemingly small application decisions.

*📺 Recording available soon at RubyEvents*

![Javier presenting about database optimization]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/caching-talk.jpg)
*Javier dramatically gestures in front of the title slide: FROM Expensive Queries TO Smart Caching A DBA's Guide for Rails*

### SAST Tooling for Ruby Applications
**Speaker:** Kylie

Sometimes a talk hits exactly the problem you were staring at. I was just figuring out security tooling for meetanother.day and Kylie's talk covered every question I had, and let me confidently close one issue and focus on the CodeQL workflows that are available for free for open-source apps on GitHub! It was a fantastic overview of SAST tooling for Ruby applications!

My thoughts: Perfect timing for me personally, and the comprehensive comparison of different security tools was exactly what I needed.

*📺 Recording available soon at RubyEvents*

![Kylie presenting about SAST tools]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/sast-talk.jpg)
*Kylie breaks down exactly which vulnerabilities each SAST tool covers in a detailed comparison table*

## Community and Connections

One of the best parts of XO Ruby Atlanta was the community aspect. The intimate size of the conference made it easy to have meaningful conversations with other attendees, and I really appreciated the welcoming atmosphere that the organizers and speakers created.

I went to dinner at Ponce City Market the night before with a few locals, and people traveling in from out of town. It was great to see my Blue Ridge Ruby friends again! The pre-conference dinner was a perfect way to reconnect with familiar faces and meet new people.

After the conference, we gathered everyone who was left and headed over to Six Feet Under for dinner, then played post-conference mini-golf at Puttshack! It was a fantastic way to end a very full day of conferencing.

![Group photo or networking moment]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/posts/xo-ruby-atlanta/community.jpg)
*A table full of Rubyists at 6 Feet Under after the event.*

## Final Thoughts

I had such an incredible time at XO Ruby today! I'm really grateful to @jremsikjr and the folks at @flagrant for putting this together. Regional conferences and the energy from in-person events can't be beat!

XO Ruby Atlanta was a fantastic event that showcased both the technical excellence and the warm community spirit that make Ruby conferences so special. The talks were insightful, the people were wonderful, and the overall experience left me feeling inspired and connected to the broader Ruby community.

If you're interested in watching any of these talks, keep an eye on [RubyEvents](https://www.rubyevents.org/events/xoruby-atlanta-2025/talks) where the recordings will be posted. And if you get a chance to attend a future XO Ruby event, I'd highly recommend it!
